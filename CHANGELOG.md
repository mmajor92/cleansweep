## v3.19
- **A third game: Line Up**, at `cleansweepdaily.fyi/lineup/`. Six things to put in order (lightest to heaviest, oldest to newest, slowest to fastest, or smallest to biggest), in up to three tries. Whatever is in the right place turns green and locks. Tap two things to swap them, or drag one: by its dots on a phone, from anywhere on the card with a mouse. A new puzzle every day, with the real numbers shown at the end, a share card of green and red squares, two practice puzzles, streaks, and a logo of five rising navy bars on the yellow tile. It uses the same facts as the other two games: any verified size, weight, speed or year of a well-known thing can be on a board. See "Line Up" in the README.
- **Points for Line Up.** Each thing scores its best result over the tries: 100 right on try 1, 60 on try 2, 35 on try 3, with part of that for a near miss (one place out 40%, two out 15%), and 100 more for a clean sweep. Every day is tagged Easy (x1), Medium (x1.25) or Hard (x1.5) above the board, and the whole score is multiplied by it, so a clean sweep is worth 700, 875 or 1,050. The result card shows the score and how it was made up, "best score" joins the stats, and the share card carries the score.
- **A clean sweep for a first-try win:** a one-line "🧹 Clean sweep! 🧹" banner and lots of brooms, soap and sparkles (the same effect is used for a streak milestone). Solved boards no longer carry a second line saying the obvious.
- **The days are harder.** The difficulty is still random for each day (not tied to the weekday), but the range is now tighter: the closest two things are 1.1 to 2.5 times apart (4 to 80 years for dates). In a simulation, an average player's clean sweep rate falls from 63% to 52% (and from 72% with five things). Those players track every clue perfectly, so real people should do worse. The kind of puzzle never repeats two days running, and **a board never repeats an earlier one**. The days are frozen in `data/lineup-days.json` (36 days to start, from 30 October 2026), and the weekly job adds more.
- **One narrow switcher under every title, instead of the games list.** Three small boxes, one for each game, each a link to it, with an icon for how today's puzzle went there (green tick, orange dot, empty ring, coral cross) and no scores or streaks. Names shorten to "Sweep" and "Mystery" on phones. The boxes are 44 px tall and sit in the same place on all three games (the Mystery Thing and Line Up titles are a little smaller on phones so the headers match).
- **Two buttons on every result card,** half the width of the card each, one for each of the other two games: yellow "Play ..." or "Finish ..." if it is not finished, an outline with its tick or cross if it is. Clean Sweep's result card has them now too.
- **Tidier "?" panels.** The long "Want to try one first?" paragraph is gone from all three (the Practice 1 and Practice 2 buttons stay, under the grey line). Mystery Thing's help is a white intro line, six short numbered steps (the arrow; green and red; Easy; Medium; Hard; use the clues and the six guesses), and a white "one try a day: your difficulty locks" line. Line Up's help drops the how-to-drag detail and splits the kinds of measure from the daily rule.
- **One copy routine for all three games** (`copyText` in `src/games.js`): the browser's clipboard first, then the older copy command with a hidden box (many in-app browsers and older phones still allow it, so more people just see "Copied"), and only if both fail a text box with the result, already selected and one line per line, just under the Copy button (before, Mystery Thing and Line Up showed run-together text above the board). A share that fails goes the same way; closing the share sheet does not.
- **Each game shows its own flame** after its puzzle number ("Puzzle #12 · 🔥 12") and on its result card, from a 2-day streak. Flames no longer appear on other games' pages, so the `streak:*` notes that carried them are gone.
- **⚡ for speed in Line Up** (not 💨, which means dust in Clean Sweep), and **a 🧹 before the address** on all three share cards.
- **New counts:** Clean Sweep `started` and `streak-7`, `-30`, `-100`; Mystery Thing `mystery-streak-7`, `-30`, `-100`; Line Up `lineup-started`, `lineup-solved`, `lineup-finished`, `lineup-clean-sweep` and `lineup-streak-7`, `-30`, `-100`; `switch-to-clean-sweep`, `switch-to-mystery`, `switch-to-lineup` for taps on a switcher box or a result-card button; and `all-three-done`, once on the day a player has finished all three games.
- **The deploy workflow changes by one word again:** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` now also copies the `lineup` folder. `npm run lint:data` also checks Line Up (the practice puzzles, the frozen days, no repeated boards), `npm run build` also writes `dist/lineup/index.html`, and the weekly job (`npm run generate:ahead`) also freezes Line Up's days.
- All unit tests pass (377), the data checks are clean, and the browser checks for the game (82), the switcher, flames, counts, buttons and layout from 320 to 430 px (74) and Mystery Thing's result card (10) pass.

## v3.8
- **A new kind of date question: "closer in time".** About one puzzle in three (35%) now has one. The card sits in the middle of the ruler with two other events as buttons, the earlier one on the left and the later one on the right, and you pick which the card is closer to ("Is the death of Cleopatra closer in time to the building of the Great Pyramid or the sinking of the Titanic?"). Both options then slide out from the card to their true distances, the nearer one is the answer, and it becomes the next card. This is the "Round B" style from the preview. It is only for dates, never question 1, never a bridge, at most 2 in a puzzle, and it needs no new facts.
- **How fair it is.** The two options are on opposite sides of the card, at least 10 years away, the farther one 1.15 to 1.5 times as far as the nearer and at least 15 years farther (so 12 years against 18 is never asked), both well known and with exact dates. Nearly nine in ten are Close calls (double points), the rest are Medium. A question is never asked twice (the card and the two options, either way round). There are about 37,800 possible ones from 147 well-known events.
- **Built to be safe.** A closer question is an ordinary question (the answer is the nearer event) plus a `closer` record, so the game's right-or-wrong check is unchanged, and an old cached copy of the game would show it as a plain "before or after?" question about the answer event, which is still correct. Puzzles 1 to 6 are untouched, and the practice puzzles never use the new question. The other option counts as being on screen (for "no object two days running") and is listed with its source on the result card.
- **Puzzles 7 to 24 were rebuilt** to include them. The data check now prints how many puzzles have one and how many are Close calls, and warns if they become rare (under 15%) or common (over 60%).
- **The README and this changelog no longer say "every question is a close call"**, which was loose: the questions run from close to fairly easy, none is more than 3 times apart, and about a third are within 1.5 times.
- Tests grew from 254 to 265 (generation, fairness, scoring, no repeats, the validator, wording, ruler positions, and a whole game played through the screens).

## v3.5
- **A question is not asked again until the others have been used.** A question is two objects compared in one category, either way round. Before this, the game only avoided yesterday's objects, so the same two things came back within weeks: in a simulated run the first repeated question came on day 17, and by the second half of the first year about 4 questions in 10 had been asked before. Now the first repeat comes on **day 388**, none of the first year's questions repeat, and year two is 25% (it was 57%) and year three 63% (it was 70%). The simulation counts the same two objects in either order. There are about 6,400 different questions, so after about a year the thin categories (weight and speed, about 480 questions each) run dry and the oldest questions come back first.
- **How it works.** The freezing script (`npm run generate`, and the Monday job) reads every saved puzzle, up to 1,100 days back, to learn which questions were asked. A new puzzle's first tries refuse any question ever asked, then any asked in the last year; after that questions are only preferred, never asked first and then asked longest ago. The refusals are lifted long before the tries run out, so a puzzle always builds. Published days never change. **The weekly pull request must be merged before the next Monday's run.**
- **What it costs, measured.** Questions are about as hard as before (typical gap between the two things compared is within a few percent in every category; weight questions are slightly easier, 2.8 times against 2.5). The share of questions on objects nearly everyone knows drops from 62% to 58%. About 8% of bridge objects go unused in a year, against 5% before, because an object with few partners uses up its few questions, so that check's limit is now 10%. Fewer objects are almost never seen (24 of 218 in the first year, from 26), and the busiest bridges are more even (15 to 16 days, from up to 24). Switches per day, warm-ups and deep cuts are unchanged.
- **The data check measures three simulated years in a row** (one long series, so the memory carries over) and averages them, instead of one year, which was so noisy that changing one weight moved four objects across the 12% line. It now prints how long before a question is asked again, and warns if the first repeat comes before day 180 or more than 5% of the first year's questions repeat. It takes about 30 seconds instead of 3.
- 10 new tests (the question key, every published puzzle's questions, a puzzle always builds even if every question was asked, the freezing script reading the saved puzzles, and the three-year behaviour) and the "rarely seen" check moved into the three-year test. Tests grew from 234 to 244.

## v3.2
- **The same question never comes two days running.** Sunday's and Monday's puzzles (#3 and #4) shared two identical questions, and the generator had nothing to stop it: across a simulated year, about 1 day in 9 repeated a fact from the day before and nearly half repeated some object. Now an object that appears in one day's puzzle cannot appear in the next day's. It cost almost nothing: questions about objects everyone knows dip by a point or two (to about 55%), and a bridge object comes back after about 27 days instead of 25. Over a simulated year there are no repeats at all.
- **Objects that appeared recently are picked less often, so the whole pool gets used.** Until now the same well-known objects came round again and again while others were almost never seen. An object that appeared yesterday is now picked at about a sixth of its normal rate, rising back to normal over 45 days (`freshDays` and `freshFloor` in `RULES`). Over a simulated year, everyday objects seen on fewer than 5 days drop from 19 to 10 (of 143), objects reused from the last 7 days drop by about a quarter, and nothing else moves: questions about objects everyone knows stay at about 55%, and warm-ups, Deep cuts and bridge returns are unchanged. It is a real but modest gain: what is left is mostly objects with a single fact (a deck of cards, days in a week), which can only appear next to a value within 4x of theirs, so the cure there is more facts near those values.
- The rule is a setting (`noRepeatDays` in `RULES`, 1 by default). It is a strict preference for the first 300 tries and then lifted as a last resort, so a puzzle always builds.
- **Puzzles 6 to 24 were rebuilt under the rule.** Puzzles 1 to 5 stay exactly as they were played or are being played. Puzzles 22 to 24 are new, so the pending "Add upcoming puzzles" pull request is not needed.
- The weekly job and the freezing script now read the previous day's saved puzzle, so the first puzzle of every new batch also avoids it. The data check fails if two saved puzzles from #5 on share an object, and checks the same over a whole simulated year. It also reports how many everyday objects appear on fewer than 5 days a year, and warns if that is more than 12%. Its "bridge objects never used" warning now scales with the size of the pool (6%) instead of a fixed count.
- Tests grew from 199 to 211, including one that runs the real freezing script and checks that switching the new rules off is caught.

## v3
- **The chain can now switch category at any point, and more than once.** Until now every puzzle was two halves joined by one bridge. A puzzle now switches category once to four times (about 34%, 43%, 18% and 4% of days), anywhere in the chain, so a stretch in one category can be a single question or as many as five. The game's look and feel are unchanged: a switch is the same glide the halfway point always had, just not always at the halfway point.
- **New rules keep it fair.** No category takes more than 5 of the 7 questions. Between 2 and 5 answers are "higher". Never more than 4 answers in a row go the same way, and any stretch of 3 or more questions has both a higher and a lower answer. Difficulty (close call, medium, easy) comes out the same as before.
- **Bridges:** a bridge object is not reused within 7 days (the generator now tries many plans before accepting a recent one, so this holds every day). Famous bridges still come round more often. Each day starts in a different category: every block of 5 days uses all five. Pacing: the day after a busy one (three or more switches) has at most two, so two wild days never come back to back.
- **The next puzzles use it from puzzle 3.** Puzzles 1 and 2 stay as they are (they are the older two-half shape, which the game still reads). Puzzles 3 to 21 were rebuilt before anyone could have played them.
- **New practice puzzles.** Practice 1 has one switch (speed, then how many, through Usain Bolt). Practice 2 has two (date, how many, then weight) and ends on a Deep cut. Both use only objects nearly everyone knows.
- **How to play** has one new sentence about the same object carrying you into a different kind of measure.
- **Clearer starting values.** A date under 1000 now shows its era ("80 AD", not a bare "80"; BC already showed), and the line under a date's big figure uses the question's own wording ("Opening of the Colosseum", "Sealing of Magna Carta") instead of a short label that was sometimes just a name. A date whose wording is too long (over 45 characters) keeps its short label. The ruler label above the dot no longer repeats "about" or "c.", since the big figure already says it, but keeps its unit so you can see the measure change at a switch. These apply to every puzzle, including the older two-half ones.
- **Under the hood:** a puzzle is now a list of "legs" (stretches in one category) joined by bridges instead of two halves and one hinge. `normalizePuzzle` converts older puzzle files on load, so they keep working. The data linter, freezing script and practice script were updated, and the tests grew from 174 to 199. They include games played through the real page with one, two and four switches, reloading the page at every point of those games, and the real puzzles 1 and 2 (still in the older shape) played, resumed part-way and finished, so nobody who is mid-game when v3 goes live loses their place.
